## Analysis

The paragraph raises a legitimate scientific question: **which comparisons between biological organisms and artificial agents are
useful, and what evidence would justify them?** Its strongest point is that a difference in material composition does not, by itself,
settle every question about agency, adaptation, intelligence, or possible experience.

Its main weakness is that it moves between several different questions: biological ancestry, the definition of life, functional
agency, and the significance of physical substrate. These questions can inform one another, but an answer to one does not settle
the others. The analysis becomes stronger when each comparison has a declared meaning and an observable test.

## 1. Where would agentic AI belong on the tree of life?

In its biological sense, the tree of life represents evolutionary relationships among organisms through shared genetic history.
It is not a general ranking of intelligence, complexity, or usefulness. The Tree of Life Web Project explicitly distinguishes
lineage relationships from misleading interpretations of evolutionary advancement. [Tree of Life Web Project][tree]

On that meaning, I would not assign an ordinary software agent to a biological branch merely because it plans, uses tools, or
resembles an organism in selected behaviors. Its engineering history and model ancestry are different relationships from the
biological descent that the tree represents. Human manufacture is not equivalent to biological reproduction.

This is a limit on the classification, not a dismissal of the comparison. An engineered agent can be compared with an organism
along dimensions such as feedback control, memory, adaptation, persistence, or dependence on an environment. Those dimensions
belong in an explicitly defined comparative framework rather than being silently substituted for biological ancestry.

It also follows that even hypothetical carbon-based life with an independent evolutionary origin would not automatically belong
within Earth's established lineages. Carbon content alone cannot establish a place on that tree. A biological component in a hybrid
system would have its own ancestry; classifying the whole engineered system would require a separately stated purpose and rule.

## 2. Does being non-carbon-based make the concept nonsense?

**No. Non-carbon composition alone is not a sufficient argument against making a scientific comparison.** But the phrase "the
concept" needs clarification. Does it mean agentic AI itself, comparing AI with organisms, or classifying a particular AI as alive?
These are different claims with different burdens of evidence.

NASA discusses a provisional working definition of life centered on a chemical system that sustains itself and can undergo
Darwinian evolution. It describes that definition as non-binding and acknowledges uncertainty about life's requirements. Carbon
is not an explicit requirement in that definition. [NASA][life]

That omission does not automatically qualify software as living. Under such a definition, one must establish the relevant chemical
organization, self-sustaining process, and evolutionary capacity. Calling a model an agent does not establish those properties.
Nor does dependence on human infrastructure, considered alone, settle the issue; the proposed system boundary and definition matter.

The defensible position is to investigate specified similarities and differences. Treating every comparison as nonsense because
the materials differ is too broad. Treating functional similarity as sufficient proof of biological life is also unsupported.

## 3. Does carbon versus silicon matter? If so, why?

### Chemistry and physical implementation

Yes, material composition can matter to the mechanisms a system can realize. Carbon and silicon are not interchangeable chemical
building blocks. A detailed assessment by Petkowski, Bains, and Seager examines silicon's chemical diversity, reactivity, and solvent
constraints. It finds substantial obstacles to predominantly silicon-based biochemistry, including silica formation in water-rich
conditions. [Petkowski, Bains, and Seager, 2020][chemistry]

Those are arguments about chemical mechanisms under specified environments. They are not a demonstration that electronic systems
cannot implement learning or goal-directed control. A computer using silicon semiconductor devices is also a different proposal
from an organism whose biochemistry primarily substitutes silicon for carbon. Conflating them makes the question harder to answer.

### Agency, intelligence, and experience

For functional comparisons, identify the property first. If the property is retaining information, adapting behavior, or pursuing
a specified objective through feedback, examine how the system implements it and how its performance changes under intervention.
Material differences may explain capabilities and limits; the material's name cannot replace that investigation.

It would be equally premature to assert that substrate never matters. A proposed capacity may depend on particular physical
processes. The scientific task is to identify those dependencies and test whether another implementation realizes the relevant
mechanism. Functional resemblance, biological life, and subjective experience require separate arguments.

The paragraph therefore works best as a challenge to justify substrate-based exclusions, rather than as an assertion that all
substrates are equivalent or that a silicon-based computer is already a silicon-based organism.

## 4. Are biologists willing to make these comparisons?

**Yes. There are documented examples, including comparisons much more specific than a general analogy between people and AI.**

### Michael Levin: diverse intelligence across embodiments

Michael Levin's research spans developmental biology, artificial life, bioengineering, and cognitive science. His laboratory
studies problem-solving in cells and tissues and seeks principles relevant to engineered and hybrid systems.
[Levin Lab research overview][levinlab]

In his 2025 perspective, *Artificial Intelligences: A Bridge Toward Diverse Intelligence and Humanity's Future*, Levin explicitly
compares biological and artificial systems within a broader study of diverse intelligence. He argues for investigating the relation
between physical embodiment and cognitive capacities rather than relying on familiar categories alone. Importantly, he does not
claim that today's popular AI systems are human minds or already implement the self-construction principles seen in biology.
[Levin, 2025][levin]

This is a direct example of a biologist making the comparison. It is a perspective and research framework, not a consensus finding
that ordinary AI agents are living organisms or sentient beings.

### Thomas S. Ray: Tierra and digital evolution

Thomas S. Ray's *Evolution, Ecology and Optimization of Digital Organisms* describes Tierra, a system of self-replicating programs
subject to mutation and competition for computational resources. Its populations exhibit evolutionary and ecological dynamics.
The paper identifies Ray with a biology department and treats digital organisms as an experimental means of studying these processes.
[Ray, Tierra][ray]

Tierra demonstrates a concrete non-biochemical setting in which specified evolutionary processes can be investigated. Its organisms
are not interchangeable with a contemporary tool-using language-model agent; the replication and selection mechanisms must be
established for the actual system being discussed.

### Richard E. Lenski and colleagues: Avida experiments

Richard E. Lenski and colleagues' 2003 Nature paper, *The evolutionary origin of complex features*, studied self-replicating digital
programs that mutate, compete, and evolve in Avida. They investigated the emergence of complex logical functions through mutation
and natural selection. [Lenski et al., 2003][lenski]

This is experimental work using a digital system to address a biological question. It establishes that comparisons of this kind
can produce testable scientific results. It does not establish that every software system is alive, or that an AI's claimed autonomy
is equivalent to biological autonomy.

These examples answer the paragraph's question affirmatively. They demonstrate active investigation of biological/artificial
comparisons, while leaving the classification and capacities of any particular agent to be established separately.

## 5. Make the comparison explicit

The following distinctions prevent the word "life-form" from carrying several untested conclusions at once. These are proposed
operational questions for evaluating a specified system; they are not a claim that one universal definition settles every case.

| Comparison | What needs to be established |
| --- | --- |
| Biological ancestry | A supported lineage relationship, rather than similarity or human manufacture. |
| Living organization | Satisfaction of a declared definition, with appropriate physical and organizational evidence. |
| Evolutionary capacity | Heritable variation and differential reproduction under specified conditions. |
| Functional agency | A defined goal, action-feedback process, and observed behavior under controlled changes. |
| Intelligence | Performance on specified learning or problem-solving tasks, including failures and transfer limits. |
| Sentience | Evidence relevant to subjective experience; task performance alone does not settle it. |

For example, copying a software file is not, by itself, evidence of an autonomous evolutionary population. Adjusting model weights
is not automatically Darwinian evolution. Successful tool use is evidence about the tested task, not proof of metabolism,
self-maintenance, biological ancestry, or subjective experience.

The appropriate comparison depends on what we want to explain. A functional classification can include biological and artificial
agents without asserting that they share an evolutionary lineage. A phylogenetic classification answers a different question.

## 6. What the paragraph does rhetorically

The paragraph challenges a potentially unjustified boundary and then converts that challenge into researchable questions. That is
its most useful move. Asking whether biologists have already investigated the comparison invites a source search rather than a
debate conducted entirely through intuition.

Two details could weaken the argument if left implicit. First, asking where AI belongs among life-forms can presuppose that its
status as a life-form has already been established. Second, calling the alternative "nonsense" can compress several distinct
positions into one dismissal. A critic may accept functional comparison while rejecting a particular biological classification.

The final sentence proposes a practical research habit: treat question marks as opportunities for an agentic investigation. In
this instance, the investigation identifies named researchers and published work. Its value comes from locating and distinguishing
evidence, rather than turning every open question into an asserted answer.

An effective instruction is: identify the claim, define the relevant terms, search primary sources, compare the strongest applicable
arguments, and report what the evidence establishes and what remains unresolved. The paragraph already contains the beginnings of
that method; it becomes more rigorous when its different questions are separated.

## 7. Connection to AIH, AITE, and successful engineering

This topic illustrates both error types discussed in the earlier documents. An AIH failure would be inventing a biologist's position
or reporting a hypothesis as an established result. An AITE failure would be silently changing a question about agency into a claim
about consciousness, or turning a functional analogy into a statement of biological ancestry.

A DTA-style analysis preserves those distinctions, the original question, plausible interpretations, and the sources attached to
each claim. A DTE-style evidence process can check declared records and evaluation rules. Neither the structure of the record nor
deterministic processing supplies missing biological or consciousness evidence.

The engineering question is consequently specific: does the agent help investigate the intended problem, preserve meaning, locate
relevant evidence, and keep conclusions within that evidence? Life or sentience is not a prerequisite for evaluating those observed
contributions. Claims about life or sentience remain separate research questions.

## 8. A more precise version of the paragraph

> You invoke biology. Which properties of agentic AI can usefully be compared with those of living organisms, and which differences
> limit that comparison? I recognize that the biological tree of life represents ancestry. Is a separate framework needed to compare
> agency, adaptation, and intelligence across biological and artificial systems?
>
> Is lack of carbon-based biochemistry a sufficient reason to reject those comparisons? Where does physical substrate matter, and
> what evidence establishes that it matters? Which biologists have investigated these questions, and what do their results support?
>
> When I put a question mark in a comment, I am inclined to ask Codex to investigate that question and return sources, distinctions,
> and unresolved issues before treating my initial assumption as an answer.

The paragraph's central challenge is credible: **differences in substrate should be connected to demonstrated mechanisms and limits,
and comparisons should be evaluated on their scientific usefulness.** The available sources justify investigating biological and
artificial systems together. They do not, on their own, justify placing ordinary agentic AI on Earth's biological tree of life.
